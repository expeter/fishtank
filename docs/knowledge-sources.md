# Field-guide research ledger

Reviewed 2026-09-27. The offline English/German species profiles live in `src/fishKnowledge.ts`. These are newly written educational summaries, not reproduced care sheets. Source links are kept here for maintainers; the children's guide does not send players to external websites.

## Scope and interpretation

- All 24 offered animal IDs have their own profile. Aquarium crab and shrimp entries explain why the same game sprite appearing in both water types is fantasy.
- A named fish's game age, permanent health, instant feeding, growth and friendly interactions are not real husbandry rules. Before a real aquarium is stocked, an adult must check its exact species, adult size, group, filtration and water needs.
- Lifespans are approximate care-planning information, not a promise or a mortality timer. Where the sprite represents a family rather than one species, the guide says so and avoids invented exact numbers. Wild and captive observations are not treated as interchangeable.
- Temperature bands are introductory, not instructions to alter existing water suddenly. Breeding strains, exact species and companion animals affect the appropriate setup. No chemical dosing, medical treatment or universal stocking formula is supplied.
- “Window snail”, “little frog”, “little crab”, “pufferfish” and “spotted ray” are broad fictional designs. The snail profile covers common freshwater aquarium snails. The frog is a pond visitor, explicitly not an African dwarf frog. The crab uses a labelled vampire-crab example, rather than pretending all crabs can stay underwater. The ray is a miniature marine fantasy animal, not a recommendation to purchase a stingray.
- The freshwater selection contains commonly encountered German aquarium species. This does not imply that every offered animal is native to Germany or suitable for a beginner.
- Each profile is short; multiple independent references support the more detailed profiles. No source's text is copied into the game. Differences between reported lifespans are reflected in qualified wording rather than a falsely exact average.

## Shared learning topics

- [Tetra: choosing suitable fish food](https://blog.tetra.net/de-de/das-richtige-fischfutter/) — different feeding zones and food formats; a commercial manufacturer's educational source, not an endorsement of a brand.
- [OATA barb husbandry](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-barbs/) — varied species-appropriate feeding, modest portions and avoiding water pollution. Its barb-specific stocking details are not generalized to all species.

### Six late-game species (reviewed 2026-09-27)

Original bilingual summaries are deliberately concise (under 200 combined English/German words per primary page). No source passages are copied. Care numbers are guidance, not a prescription for every individual; precise lifespan is omitted where these sources do not establish one. Game prices, growth, breeding and mixed habitats are fictional.

- Pearl gourami: [Aqueon care guide](https://www.aqueon.com/resources/care-guides/gouramis) and [manufacturer care sheet](https://www.aqueon.com/-/media/project/oneweb/aqueon/files/us/care-sheets/fish/aq_gourami_cs_mar20b.pdf): labyrinth breathing, omnivory, planted shelter, 30 US gallon recommendation for pearl gouramis (rounded to 115 L). [US Fish and Wildlife Service](https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Ecological-Risk-Screening-Summary-Pearl-Gourami.pdf) documents species identity and Southeast Asian distribution.
- Boesemani rainbowfish: [Aquarium Co-Op's own husbandry guide](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/boesemani-rainbowfish): 5–8 years, 8–10 cm, group of six, 120 cm swimming length, 24–28 °C, pH 6–8, varied small foods and juvenile colour development.
- Black ghost knifefish: [Aquarium Co-Op's own husbandry experience](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/black-ghost-knifefish): over a decade, 35–45 cm, 120–180 US gallons (roughly 450–680 L), 24–28 °C, pH 6.8–7.8, weak electrolocation, territoriality and predation on small animals.
- Regal angelfish: [Maidenhead Aquatics husbandry guide](https://www.fishkeeper.co.uk/fish/marine/angelfish-dwarf-angelfish/regal-angelfish): specialist sponge-feeding diet, 25 cm, 23–27 °C, pH 8.1–8.4, solitary housing, coral nipping and feeding difficulty.
- Achilles tang: [Abyss Aquatics' livestock care information](https://www.abyssaquatics.co.uk/product/achilles-tang/) supplies temperature, pH and strong flow; [Ocean To You's own conditioning and care guidance](https://oceantoyou.com/collections/tangs/products/achilles-tang) supplies algae diet, expert care, large established system and territoriality. No precise age is claimed.
- Zebra shark: [National Aquarium's animal page and caretaker observations](https://aqua.org/explore/animals/zebra-shark) supplies juvenile stripes/adult spots, size, nocturnal diet and tail; [Aquarium of the Pacific's 2018 visitor guide](https://www.aquariumofpacific.org/downloads/2018VisitorGuide20AnivENG.pdf) documents Fern over twenty years old. [Its original breeding research](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.886616/full) uses the updated scientific name _Stegostoma tigrinum_ and describes professional-scale facilities. The guide explicitly excludes this species from home aquariums.

### Expanded discovery chapters (reviewed 2026-09-27)

All 30 profiles now have seven additional original bilingual passages: native habitat, adult size, body/senses, lifecycle, wild diet, behaviour and common care mistakes. These complement the existing profiles. Sizes are approximate adult guidance; generic game animals deliberately retain family/species variation. The snail is not assigned an invented universal breeding method; the crab example remains the largely terrestrial vampire crab; the frog remains a pond visitor. Reef butterflyfish, rays, puffers and seahorses are not falsely treated as one precise species. Aquarium suitability is kept separate from fictional game placement.
